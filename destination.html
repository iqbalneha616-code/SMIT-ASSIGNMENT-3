Top destinations in Pakistan



  1. Hunza Valley


    2. Hunza Valley is a breathtaking mountain valley in northern Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan, often called the "Switzerland of Pakistan." Nestled along the Karakoram Highway near the borders of China and Afghanistan, it’s surrounded by some of the world’s highest peaks including Rakaposhi, Ultar Sar, and the iconic Ladyfinger Peak. The valley is famous for its turquoise rivers, terraced apricot orchards, and centuries-old forts like Baltit and Altit that overlook Karimabad, the main town. Known for its vibrant Burusho culture, hospitable people, and stunning autumn colors, Hunza also has a reputation for longevity and clean living. With glaciers, lakes like Attabad, and views of Passu Cones, it feels almost otherworldly, and it’s a favorite for trekkers, photographers, and anyone chasing that mix of adventure and peace.


  2. Skardu


    3. Skardu is a stunning high-altitude town in Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan, often called the gateway to some of the world’s greatest mountains. Surrounded by the Karakoram, Himalaya, and Ladakh ranges, it sits at about 2,228 meters along the Indus River and offers views of peaks like K2, Gasherbrum, and the Masherbrum Massif. The town is known for its dramatic landscapes: turquoise lakes like Upper Kachura and Shangrila Lake, the cold desert of Katpana with its sand dunes against snow peaks, and historic sites like Skardu Fort and the 16th-century Kharpocho Fort overlooking the valley. Culturally rich with Balti heritage, Skardu is also the main base for expeditions to the Baltoro Glacier and the Concordia. Whether you’re into trekking, mountaineering, or just soaking in the crisp air and starry nights, Skardu feels raw, remote, and unforgettable.


  3. Lahore


    4. Lahore is the heart and soul of Pakistan, a city where history, culture, and chaos all collide in the most vibrant way. As the capital of Punjab, it’s famous for its Mughal-era landmarks like the Lahore Fort, Badshahi Mosque, and the winding streets of the Walled City with its colorful bazaars and food stalls. The city runs on poetry, music, and flavor — from sizzling BBQ on Gawalmandi Food Street to late-night chai and qawwali at Data Darbar. Lahore blends old and new effortlessly: rickshaws and horse carts share roads with modern cafes, universities, and malls, while festivals like Basant and Eid light up the streets. Known as the cultural capital, Lahoris are proud, loud, and famously hospitable, making the city feel alive 24/7.


  4. Karachi


    5. Karachi is Pakistan’s largest city and its economic heartbeat, a sprawling coastal metropolis on the Arabian Sea that never really sleeps. It’s a city of contrasts: high-rise offices in Clifton and DHA sit alongside historic neighborhoods like Saddar with colonial buildings, while bustling markets like Empress Market and Tariq Road buzz with life 24/7. As the country’s main port and business hub, Karachi is home to people from every ethnicity, language, and background, which is why its food scene is legendary — from Burns Road nihari and bun kebabs to seaside do darya cafes and late-night chai. The beaches at Clifton and Hawksbay, the mausoleum of Quaid-e-Azam, and the energy of the traffic, art, and music all give Karachi its unique, fast-paced, slightly chaotic charm. It’s messy and loud, but also full of ambition, opportunity, and resilience. Back to top
